## Finding 1 — Remaining blockers

1. **P1: cleanup can SIGKILL an unrelated process after PID reuse.** `crates/tide-demo/src/lib.rs:1236–1246` collects numeric PIDs from `/proc`, then launches a separate `kill -KILL PID...` process. A descendant can exit and be reaped after its stat file was read and before kill resolves its PID; another same-user process can acquire that number and receive SIGKILL. The later group rescan cannot undo that kill. This applies to descendants even while the direct child's PID remains reserved. Reaping the leader at line 1174 also releases the retained numeric group identity once the original group disappears. This is a newly introduced safety defect in the attempted fix, within finding 1's cleanup scope. The [Linux pidfd_send_signal documentation](https://www.man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/pidfd_send_signal.2.html) describes this exact numeric-PID signaling race. Minimum correction: use signaling with a stable target identity, or actual group signaling while keeping its identity reserved through cleanup; a second stat check followed by numeric kill still has the race.

2. **P2: success can still be accepted after the deadline.** At `lib.rs:1171–1180`, the clock is checked before `try_wait` and a complete `/proc` scan, but never after them before returning success. With EOF already seen, a remaining group member may close its streams and exit after the deadline while this thread is descheduled or scanning `/proc`. The scan can then find no running members and return `Some(status)` after expiry. Thus the report's claim that all three conditions hold before the deadline is stronger than the code. Minimum correction: re-check the deadline after the completion observations and before accepting success, routing expiry through the timeout path. A passing one-second timeout test does not cover this boundary.

3. **P2: descendants are killed but are not reaped or proved absent.** `group_members` explicitly filters out state `Z` (`lib.rs:1224`). `run_bounded` waits only for its direct `Child` (`1174`, `1193–1195`); it neither adopts nor waits for orphaned descendants. Killing the subshell and sleep together in the new regression can leave their zombies with init or an ancestor subreaper. The test's `still_running` helper explicitly accepts those zombies (`tests/demo.rs:92–106`), so passing it does not prove an empty process group or no zombie leak. A functioning external reaper may eventually remove them, but this code does not own or verify that outcome. The [Linux subreaper documentation](https://www.man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/PR_SET_CHILD_SUBREAPER.2const.html) explains adoption and the corresponding ability to wait for orphaned descendants. Minimum correction: give descendant reaping an explicit owner, scoped to this invocation, and verify actual cleanup rather than treating zombies as absence. Do not introduce a process-wide waiter that can consume unrelated concurrent tests' children.

4. **P2: the cleanup time limit is not enforced around its blocking operation, and cleanup failure is hidden.** `kill_group` checks its five-second limit only between iterations, then calls the PATH-selected external `kill` with unbounded `.status()` (`lib.rs:1240–1246`). A stalled kill executable or wrapper hangs this helper indefinitely; the original debugger-hang defect has moved into cleanup. If kill instead fails promptly, its error/status is discarded, and after five seconds `kill_group` returns the same `()` whether the group is empty or still has survivors (`1237–1238`). `run_bounded` can then return with survivors, while step 5 says the group was killed. For example, a PATH-selected kill that exits unsuccessfully leaves an early-exited command's sleeping descendant alive; no direct-child kill can reach it. Minimum correction: bound or eliminate this external blocking wait, propagate cleanup failure, and establish the required cleanup outcome before claiming it. The pipe-reader thread is also not joined and can remain blocked on a surviving writer after the two-second drain ends.
